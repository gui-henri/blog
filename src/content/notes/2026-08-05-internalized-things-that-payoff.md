We end up internalizing behaviors without even noticing, including at software engineering. How many of us had read the Agile Manifesto? How many of us internalized it in our actions and day-to-day? At some point in your carreer, you may be questioned about why you did something the way you did, what were the advantages and disadvantages. Sometimes, the task was really hard and you had to put your thought there, but most of the times, it is not. And in the case it's not, you need to be able to back yourself, because answering why it was done the way it was in a satisfactory way will be very hard. Among other reasons, the main one for this is that you probably were on *autopilot* when you performed the action.

I'm still in the proccess of shaping my way of thinking (AI turned things just when i was about to reach my own conclusions about some topics), but i do believe that rationalizing and understanding theory is one of the steps to seniority. When we are at our 9 to 5, we rarely try to justify our every single action. Most of our time we spend at *autopilot*, so we can focus our thougths at the hardest problems. When you're at that state, it's when you need to rationalize your behavior the most, because you may be doing something irrational without even noticing.

To fix this, we usually try to create the correct habits by making peaple read from the experience of others, effectively giving an headstart by giving the correct information from the beggining of the learning proccess. You can read the Agile Manifesto and use it as a set of theorys (guidelines, teachings, whatever) that you can clearly use guide yourself towards a goal, see when you're deviating from the path and how to calibrate your behavior. When you have truly internalized the knowledge, competence emerges, and it becomes a good behavior that you do even when on *autopilot*. It's surely a better path than knocking yourself in the head time and time again to reach the same conclusions.

Experience is not wisdom, having the years is not worth it if you took all those years to reach the same conclusions someone had after reading 5 pages of a book. Also, a good theory backing the practice will give you precision. You will be able to clearly say *why* were having X over Y, not just because *"i did like this in the past and it did or didn't worked"*. You can always reach the same conclusions for yourself, but probably a theory backing it up already exists.
