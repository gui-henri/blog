I keep a rule, and this blog exists to enforce it:

> **Each hour of study should ideally leave an artifact. Knowledge that produces nothing
> tends to disappear.**

I've watched myself forget things I "knew" — because knowing was the only artifact. No note,
no write-up, no code, no post. Just the warm feeling of having read something, which fades
faster than I'd like to admit.

## Passive learning is a leak

Read a book without notes: in a month, what survives? The plot, maybe a single idea that
happened to stick. The rest is gone.

Watch a course without building: in a week, what survives? The demo from the video, if it was
impressive. The mechanics behind it? Gone.

The pattern isn't that I didn't learn. It's that **nothing survived the hour**. Learning
without an artifact is spending the hour twice — once to learn, and once more, later, to
rediscover.

## The artifact is the receipt

An artifact is proof the hour happened. It doesn't have to be good:

- A capture note with three bullets is an artifact.
- A failing piece of code is an artifact.
- A one-paragraph "I studied X and hit a weird bug" post is an artifact.

The bar is *something survived*. Once the bar is that low, it's easy to clear — and clearing
it consistently is what compounds.

## Where this blog fits

A blog is the durable end of that pipeline. Notes and posts have short lives; a long-form
essay is a chunk of thinking that stays findable for years. Five years from now, someone can
search, find this, and see how I think — and trace it back to the projects I built and the
things I shipped.

It's the record. Not the highlight reel — the record.

## The pipeline, in one breath

1. **Capture** everything the moment it appears (a 10-minute note).
2. **Internalize** what's worth keeping by writing it properly (an essay).
3. **Distribute** the rest as smaller artifacts on other channels — a thread, a post, a
   carousel, a short video.

Writing is how I learn; shipping is how I keep it. The method is documented here in the
repo, in [`content-method/`](https://github.com/gui-henri/blog/tree/main/content-method),
and it's open for anyone to steal.

## What to do with this rule

Pick your own artifact. It doesn't need to be a blog — a notebook, a gist, a folder of
half-finished sketches. What matters is that, most days, something survives the hour.

Because knowledge that produces nothing tends to disappear. And knowledge that produces
something — tends to build.
