# Content Method

A framework for turning study time into durable, distributed artifacts.

> **The maxim**
>
> Each hour of study should ideally leave an artifact.
> Knowledge that produces nothing tends to disappear.

## Why

Passive learning evaporates. If a study session ends with nothing that survives it, the
knowledge was probably lost. This method makes every session end with something concrete —
a note, a sketch, a post, a piece of code — and routes that artifact toward the places where
it builds a reputation over time.

## The three phases

```text
 IDENTIFY ──► INTERNALIZE ──► DISTRIBUTE
 (capture)    (deep work)     (artifacts)
    │              │                │
 10 minutes     hours/weeks    minutes/hours
```

| Phase | What happens | Artifact | Cadence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1 · Identify** | Capture knowledge, ideas, learnings, and current discussions the moment they appear. | A capture note in the `inbox/` collection. | As it happens (5–10 min) |
| **2 · Internalize** | Promote promising captures into essays. Writing is how I learn. | A blog essay + a video script. | Weekly review + focused sessions |
| **3 · Distribute** | Every piece of knowledge becomes an artifact on at least one channel. Publish *during* the process, not only after. | Posts on X, LinkedIn, Instagram, YouTube. | Each session |

## The one rule per session

> Before you close the laptop: **something must survive the hour.**

If you studied, capture a note. If you learned, write it down. If you built, ship it. If
nothing is ready, at least leave a note saying "still not ready — here's where it stands."
